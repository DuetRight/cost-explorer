01
Direct swap

Acrylic or solid-surface

Range
$7,000–$12,000
Typically 3–5 working days

Includes

  • Tub out, one-piece or multi-piece shower in — same footprint, same drain
  • New valve trim, glass door or curtain rod, patch and paint
  • Permanent, code-compliant acrylic or solid-surface unit

Not included

  • A tiled shower or new waterproofing membrane
  • Moving the drain or changing the footprint
  • Permit fees when a jurisdiction requires them

What moves the number

  • Unit quality — acrylic vs solid-surface
  • Valve and glass choices
  • Patch-and-paint scope after the swap
A liner installed over an existing tub in a day is a different product, with a different lifespan, than a conversion.
02
Tiled walk-in

Same footprint

Range
$15,000–$22,000
Typically 2–3 weeks

Includes

  • Full waterproofing system and sloped pan
  • Floor-to-ceiling tile, niche, pressure-balancing valve
  • Semi-frameless glass panel

Not included

  • A curbless / zero-threshold floor
  • Fixture moves or a larger footprint
  • Permit fees — a plumbing permit is usually required, and sits outside this band

What moves the number

  • Tile choice and wall area
  • Waterproofing system complexity
  • Glass and valve upgrades
The tile isn’t what fails — the membrane underneath is. Most of the labor happens before you ever see tile.
03
Curbless

Zero-threshold

Range
$20,000–$32,000
Typically 3–4 weeks

Includes

  • Recessed or re-framed subfloor for a flush shower floor
  • Linear drain, extended waterproofing, larger glass
  • Bench and grab-bar blocking

Not included

  • A full-bath renovation (vanity, toilet, flooring outside the shower)
  • Design retainers or structural surprises in the floor system
  • Building-permit fees where framing changes

What moves the number

  • How much the subfloor needs to drop
  • Waterproofing extent
  • Glass size and finish level
A flush floor is the accessibility move — and it usually looks cleaner, too.
04
Full renovation

Conversion inside a full renovation

Range
$26,000–$45,000+
Typically 4–6 weeks

Includes

  • The conversion plus flooring, vanity, toilet, lighting
  • Exhaust ventilation, electrical, and finishes
  • One crew and one timeline for the wet room

Not included

  • Expanding the room or a major layout redesign
  • Designer fixtures or custom millwork beyond this band
  • Permit fees — plumbing and electrical permits are typical, and sit outside this band

What moves the number

  • Finish level across the whole room
  • Fixture moves and waterproofing
  • Electrical and ventilation upgrades
Doing the conversion inside a full renovation keeps one crew and one timeline — instead of two projects a year apart.
